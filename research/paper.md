# The Sonnet as System
## Computational Poetics, Crisis, and the Puzzle-Logic of Shakespeare's Form

**Status:** Working research paper. The full existing writing sample is preserved separately in its original document; this Markdown file is a research-facing working outline and abstract, not a transcription or a replacement of that draft.

## Working abstract (adapted from the existing writing sample; provisional)
Shakespeare's sonnets bring rule-governed poetic structures into contact with interpretive openness. Taking Sonnet 17 as a case study, this project investigates what happens when meter, rhyme, rhetorical movement, and textual relationships are translated into playable computational constraints. Rather than assuming that the sonnet is a puzzle with a single solution, it asks which aspects of poetic form can be operationalized under explicit historical and editorial assumptions, what kinds of reasoning that interaction invites, and where formal feedback fails to settle literary meaning. *In Countless Shadows* functions as a research instrument as well as a playable artwork. Through iterative prototyping and documented observations of readers, the project examines whether computational manipulation changes the processes of literary interpretation, rather than presuming that it improves them.

## Research question
What happens to literary interpretation when poetic constraints are translated into computationally enforceable rules, and how do players negotiate the difference between formal solvability and interpretive ambiguity?

## Claims inherited from the earlier draft (to investigate, not assume)
- A sonnet's formal constraints can be modeled as a structured possibility space.
- Meaning emerges through the interaction of constraint and ambiguity, not from form alone.
- Computational play might make interpretive procedures visible and revisable.
- Making procedures visible might redistribute interpretive authority; alternatively, the software may encode a new authority.

## Advisor critique (Stephanie, November 4, 2026)
1. Distinguish the scholarly question and method from the artistic success of the game.
2. Avoid treating the prototype as a demonstration of an existing theory, especially Palfrey's.
3. Study the historical conditions of literary interpretation, pedagogy, riddles, recitation, and literacy.
4. Define what counts as knowledge from solving a literary puzzle.
5. Ground contemporary literacy and AI-mediated reading claims historically and empirically.
6. Investigate the tension between determinate puzzle solutions and open interpretation.

## Methodology to develop
- Historical and theoretical study of reading practices and media transformations.
- Close reading of Sonnet 17, with explicit attention to editorial and prosodic assumptions.
- Research-through-design: small competing mechanical prototypes, documented decisions, and falsifiable questions.
- Qualitative playtest observations: player actions, hypotheses, explanations, disagreements, and moments of uncertainty.
- If comparing reading and manipulation conditions, account for ordering effects and avoid claiming causal findings from informal playtests.

## Research questions for experiments
1. Does manipulating a poetic constraint change the kind of explanation a player offers?
2. When a configuration is formally valid, how do players handle competing readings?
3. Which interpretive possibilities become visible, and which are suppressed, by the encoded model?
4. Can the system expose tensions or contradictions not obvious from the initial close reading?
5. How can findings be evaluated without treating puzzle scores as literary understanding?
